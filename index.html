Summary

A premium store with a hidden cost and an uncertain fit.

Oura's site looks expensive and converts on aesthetics. But three things quietly cost conversions: the mandatory $5.99/mo membership is buried below the fold while the headline price reads "$349," buyers can't confirm their ring size without ordering a separate sizing kit, and the hero leads with a poetic slogan ("Subtle. Power.") instead of telling a first-time visitor what the product does. One accessibility bug: on /sizing, the Oura Ring 5 "Sizing Guide" button carries the accessible label "Sizing Guide for Oura Ring 4."

The 13 Design Questions

Reading the purchase flow

01 · OBJECTIVE
What does this flow solve?
Convert a health-curious visitor into a ring buyer, then into a recurring membership subscriber. Hardware is the hook; the subscription is the business.
02 · WHO
Who is it for?
A 28–45 health optimizer with disposable income. Already tracks something (Apple Watch, Whoop, scale). Wants insight without a screen on their wrist.
03 · WHEN / WHY
The trigger moment
Saw it on a podcaster, coworker, or "Best Inventions" list. Arrives to answer one question: "Is it worth it, and will it fit me?"
04 · MINDSET
What are they thinking?
Skeptical of cost and lock-in. Burned before by abandoned gadgets. Quietly worried it won't fit and returns will be a hassle.
05 · HOW THEY GOT HERE
The previous step
A review article, an influencer link, or a direct search. They land hot but uncommitted, comparing against Whoop and Apple Watch in another tab.
06 · FEELING
What should they feel?
"This is premium, trustworthy, and made for me." The site nails premium. It under-delivers on trustworthy because cost is partially hidden.
07 · ALTERNATIVE
Without this?
Buy a Whoop (subscription bundled, no fit anxiety) or an Apple Watch (no subscription). Both make the total cost obvious upfront.
08 · NEXT STEP
What do they do next?
Pick a finish and size, then check out. The size step is where momentum dies: there's no way to know your size on the spot.
09 · BETTER THAN EXISTING?
Confident it's better?
The product is class-leading. The purchase flow is mid: competitors are more honest about total cost and fit upfront.
10 · REMOVE
What can we cut?
The vague "Subtle. Power." hero copy and the duplicate "Explore/Shop" buttons. Say what it does and how much it truly costs.
11 · NO CONSTRAINTS
Design it fresh?
Bundle ring + first year in one transparent price, ship a free sizing kit by default, and lead with the outcome (better sleep), not a slogan.
12 · VALUE OBVIOUS?
Will users get it?
The health value, yes. The true cost and the fit path, no, until after they're emotionally committed. That's where trust leaks.
13 · GROWTH / ENG / RETENTION
Which metric?
Growth (first purchase). But hiding the subscription trades a short-term conversion bump for refund risk and churn later.

Before / After

The three highest-impact fixes

"Before" shots are the live ouraring.com pages captured June 2026. "After" shots are the proposed fixes, rendered as working mockups.

Issue 1

Hidden total cost

Why it matters: Violates Help & Documentation (H10) and erodes trust (Q6). The headline reads "$349," but the membership at $5.99/mo is required to get the insights the whole product promises. Year-one reality is ~$415, disclosed only in fine print far below the buy button. Buyers discover the recurring charge after committing, which drives refunds and 1-star "hidden fee" reviews.

Expected impact: fewer refunds, higher membership retention, more trust at checkout.
Before — live site
!"From $349" is the only price near the CTA. The required membership is disclosed far below the fold.
After — proposed
✓A cost-breakdown card shows the ~$415 year-one total at the buy button, with "cancel anytime."
Issue 2

The size dead end

Why it matters: Violates Error Prevention (H5) and User Control (H3). A ring must fit, but a buyer reaches the size step with no way to know their size on the spot. Fear of buying the wrong size and dealing with returns is a silent conversion killer. The page also carries an accessibility bug: in the Oura Ring 5 section the "Sizing Guide" button's accessible label (what a screen reader announces) reads "Sizing Guide for Oura Ring 4," the wrong product.

Expected impact: higher add-to-cart rate, fewer wrong-size returns.
Before — live site
!"Sizing Guide" leads to instructions, not an on-the-spot answer; the Ring 5 button's accessible label names Ring 4.
After — proposed
✓Inline size finder, free sizing kit, printable guide, and a 30-day fit guarantee to remove return fear.
Issue 3

A hero that hides the point

Why it matters: Violates Match to Real World (H2) and weak on first impression (Q1, Q12). "Subtle. Power." is a slogan, not a value proposition. A first-time visitor who arrived from a podcast mention should learn what the product does in one second. Instead the value prop ("collect deeply personal health metrics") sits below the fold, and a full-width cookie banner sits across the screen on load.

Expected impact: lower bounce, faster time-to-wow, clearer comparison against Whoop / Apple Watch.
Before — live site
!"Subtle. Power." says nothing about what it does, and there's no primary CTA in the hero.
After — proposed
✓Outcome-led headline, one clear CTA, social proof above the fold, cookie consent demoted to a slim bottom bar.
